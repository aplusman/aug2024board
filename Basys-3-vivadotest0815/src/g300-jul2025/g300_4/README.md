## opaque

Let's break down the word "opaque" for your GRE preparation.

---

### **Meaning of "Opaque"**

The word "opaque" (pronounced oh-PAYK) has two primary meanings:

1.  **Literal Meaning (Physical):**
    *   **Definition:** Not allowing light to pass through; not transparent or translucent. Something opaque blocks light completely, so you cannot see through it.
    *   **Analogy:** Think of a brick wall, a very thick fog, or a painted window. You can't see what's on the other side.
    *   **Opposite:** Transparent (clear, allowing light to pass through) or Translucent (allowing some light to pass through, but not clearly visible, like frosted glass).

2.  **Figurative Meaning (Abstract):**
    *   **Definition:** Difficult to understand or explain; obscure; unclear; impenetrable to the mind.
    *   **Analogy:** Think of jargon-filled legal documents, complex philosophical concepts, or a convoluted explanation. It's hard to "see through" or grasp the meaning.

**Etymology Note:** The word comes from the Latin "opacus," meaning "dark, shaded." This reinforces its core meaning related to light and clarity.

---

### **Common Collocations for "Opaque"**

Collocations are words that frequently go together. Knowing these helps you use the word naturally and recognize it in various contexts on the GRE.

**For the Literal Meaning:**

*   **Opaque glass / window / screen:** Refers to glass or surfaces that block visibility.
*   **Opaque curtain / material / fabric:** Materials designed not to be see-through.
*   **Opaque substance / liquid:** A fluid or material that is not clear.
*   **Opaque layer / film / coating:** A covering that is not transparent.
*   **Become opaque / Render opaque:** To make something lose its transparency.

**For the Figurative Meaning:**

*   **Opaque language / jargon / prose / writing:** Language that is difficult to comprehend.
*   **Opaque explanation / statement / meaning:** An explanation or concept that is unclear or hard to grasp.
*   **Opaque system / process / structure:** A system that is not transparent in its operations, often implying a lack of accountability or clarity.
*   **Opaque rules / regulations:** Rules that are hard to interpret or follow.
*   **Remain opaque / appear opaque:** To continue to be or seem unclear.

---

### **Example Sentences in Various Contexts**

Here are multiple example sentences demonstrating both meanings, along with their Traditional Chinese translations:

1.  **Literal (Physical Object - Privacy):**
    *   **English:** The bathroom window was made of **opaque glass** to ensure privacy.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 浴室窗戶採用**不透明玻璃**，以確保隱私。
    *   *(Yùshì chuānghù cǎiyòng bù tòumíng bōlí, yǐ quèbǎo yǐnsī.)*

2.  **Literal (Substance/Liquid - Description):**
    *   **English:** Shake the bottle well; the liquid inside is an **opaque white** suspension.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 請搖勻瓶子；裡面的液體是**不透明的白色**懸浮液。
    *   *(Qǐng yáoyún píngzi; lǐmiàn de yè tǐ shì bù tòumíng de bái sè xuánfúyè.)*

3.  **Figurative (Language/Writing - Difficulty in Understanding):**
    *   **English:** His writing style is often so **opaque** that it's difficult to grasp his intended message.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 他的寫作風格常常**晦澀難懂**，讓人難以掌握他想表達的訊息。
    *   *(Tā de xiězuò fēnggé chángcháng huìsè nándǒng, ràng rén nányǐ zhǎngwò tā xiǎng biáodá de xùnxī.)*

4.  **Figurative (System/Process - Lack of Transparency/Accountability):**
    *   **English:** The company's accounting practices remained **opaque** to outside auditors for years, raising suspicions.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 這家公司的會計實務多年來對外部審計師來說一直**不透明**，引發了許多疑慮。
    *   *(Zhè jiā gōngsī de kuàijì shíwù duōniánlái duì wàibù shěnjìshī lái shuō yīzhí bù tòumíng, yǐnfā le xǔduō yílǜ.)*

5.  **Figurative (Concept/Idea - Intellectual Difficulty):**
    *   **English:** Despite the professor's lengthy explanation, the philosophical concept remained **opaque** to many students.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 儘管教授冗長的解釋，這個哲學概念對許多學生來說仍然**模糊不清**。
    *   *(Jǐnguǎn jiàoshòu rǒngcháng de jiěshì, zhège zhéxué gàiniàn duì xǔduō xuéshēng lái shuō réngrán móhú bùqīng.)*

6.  **Figurative (Abstract - Meaning/Purpose):**
    *   **English:** The ancient text was so **opaque** that scholars debated its meaning for centuries without a definitive conclusion.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 這段古老文本的意義如此**晦澀**，以至於學者們爭論了幾個世紀也沒有定論。
    *   *(Zhè duàn gǔlǎo wénběn de yìyì rúcǐ huìsè, yǐ zhìyú xuézhěmen zhēnglùn le jǐ gè shìjì yě méiyǒu dìnglùn.)*

---

Understanding both the literal and figurative meanings of "opaque" is key for the GRE, as the test often features words used in their more nuanced or abstract senses. Good luck with your studies!

## denigrate

Okay, let's break down the word "denigrate" for your GRE studies.

---

### **1. Meaning of "Denigrate"**

**Denigrate** (verb) means to criticize unfairly; to belittle; to disparage; to speak damagingly about someone or something. It implies an attempt to sully or blacken someone's reputation, character, or achievements, often with an intent to make them seem less valuable or morally bad.

*   **Etymology Tip (helpful for GRE context):** The word comes from Latin *denigrare*, meaning "to blacken." This helps to remember its core meaning: to metaphorically "blacken" someone's name or reputation.

*   **Key Nuance:** It's stronger than just "criticize." It suggests a deliberate effort to lower someone's standing or esteem in the eyes of others, often unjustly.

---

### **2. Common Collocation**

The most common collocation for "denigrate" is:
*   **Denigrate someone's character/reputation/achievements.**

You'll often see it used in phrases like:
*   "attempt to denigrate"
*   "seek to denigrate"
*   "denigrate a person/group/idea"

---

### **3. Example Sentences in Various Contexts**

Here are multiple example sentences with their Traditional Chinese translations:

---

**Example 1: Political Context (Disparaging an Opponent)**

    *   *(Note: 詆毀 (dǐhuǐ) is a strong word meaning to slander or defame, fitting for an attempt to damage reputation.)*

**Example 2: Workplace/Professional Context (Belittling a Colleague's Work)**

*   **English:** It's unprofessional to **denigrate** a colleague's achievements just because you feel competitive.
*   **Traditional Chinese:** 僅僅因為競爭心態就**貶低**同事的成就，這是不專業的行為。

**Example 3: Social/Historical Context (Portraying a Group Negatively)**

    *   *(Note: Again, 貶低 works well here to convey the idea of making a group seem less worthy or capable.)*

---

**Example 4: Interpersonal Relationships (Damaging Character)**

*   **English:** He tried to **denigrate** her character by spreading false rumors, but his efforts backfired.
*   **Traditional Chinese:** 他試圖透過散佈不實謠言來**詆毀**她的品格，但他的努力卻適得其反。
    *   *(Note: 詆毀 (dǐhuǐ) is perfect here as it directly relates to damaging someone's moral character or reputation with lies.)*

---

**Example 5: Critical/Artistic Context (Unfair Criticism)**

*   **English:** Some critics unfairly **denigrate** modern art, claiming it lacks technique and meaning.
*   **Traditional Chinese:** 一些評論家不公平地**貶低**現代藝術，聲稱其缺乏技巧與意義。
    *   *(Note: 貶低 (biǎndī) works to describe the act of unfairly reducing the perceived value or quality of something.)*

**Example 6: Figurative/Abstract Context (Undermining an Idea)**

*   **English:** Don't let others **denigrate** your dreams; believe in your own potential.
*   **Traditional Chinese:** 不要讓別人**貶低**你的夢想；要相信自己的潛力。
    *   *(Note: 貶低 (biǎndī) is used here to mean undermining or making someone feel their dreams/ambitions are worthless.)*

---

**Key Takeaway for GRE:** "Denigrate" is a strong verb indicating a deliberate and often unfair attempt to damage someone's reputation, belittle their achievements, or make them seem inferior. Its negative connotation is always present.

### Malleable (adj.)

**Meaning:**

1.  **Literal (Primary):** Capable of being extended or shaped by hammering or by pressure from rollers. Easily hammered or pressed into shape without breaking or cracking. This is most commonly applied to metals.
    *   *Think:* Gold, which can be flattened into incredibly thin sheets.

2.  **Figurative (Very Common for GRE):** Easily influenced, trained, or controlled; adaptable or flexible. Ready to be shaped or formed, often in terms of ideas, character, or systems.
    *   *Think:* A young person's mind, public opinion, or a new policy.

**Etymology Note:** The word "malleable" comes from the Latin "malleus," meaning "hammer" (as in "mallet"). This connection helps reinforce the literal meaning of being shaped by force.

**Common Collocations:**

When studying collocations, think about what kinds of things are *typically* described as malleable, both literally and figuratively.

*   **Literal:**
    *   **Malleable metal:** (e.g., gold, copper, iron)
    *   **Malleable iron:** (a specific type of cast iron)
    *   **Malleable material:** (e.g., clay, plastic)

*   **Figurative:**
    *   **Malleable mind / brain:** (easily influenced, open to new ideas)
    *   **Malleable personality / character:** (adaptable, easily changed or molded)
    *   **Malleable public opinion:** (easily swayed by propaganda or events)
    *   **Malleable policy / system / rules:** (flexible, adaptable to changing circumstances)
    *   **Malleable data / evidence:** (can be interpreted or manipulated in various ways, sometimes with a negative connotation)
    *   **Malleable child / student:** (easily taught or guided)
    *   **Malleable organization / structure:** (adaptable, can change quickly)

## pessimism

That's an excellent word to understand for the GRE, as it relates to both vocabulary and reading comprehension!

Let's break down "pessimism."

---

### Meaning of "Pessimism"

**Pessimism** (noun) is a state of mind or a tendency to expect the worst possible outcome, or to dwell on the most negative aspects of a situation. It's characterized by a lack of hope, confidence, and a generally negative outlook on life, specific events, or the future.

*   **Origin:** The word comes from the Latin "pessimus," meaning "worst."
*   **Antonym:** Its direct opposite is **optimism**, which is the tendency to expect the best and be hopeful.

In simpler terms, a pessimist always sees the glass half-empty and often believes that things will go wrong, even when there's evidence to the contrary.

---

### Common Collocations of "Pessimism"

Collocations are words that often go together. Knowing these will make your writing and speaking sound more natural and sophisticated.

**Verbs that often precede or relate to "pessimism":**
*   **express/show/display** pessimism (to outwardly show it)
*   **feel/experience** pessimism (to inwardly have it)
*   **succumb to/fall into/give in to** pessimism (to be overcome by it)
*   **combat/overcome/fight** pessimism (to try to defeat it)
*   **fuel/contribute to** pessimism (to cause or increase it)
*   **border on** pessimism (to be very close to it, often in an extreme way)

**Adjectives that often describe "pessimism":**
*   **deep/profound** pessimism (very strong)
*   **growing/widespread** pessimism (increasing or affecting many people)
*   **unbridled/rampant** pessimism (uncontrolled or spreading unchecked)
*   **general/constant** pessimism (a consistent, overall negative outlook)
*   **excessive/unwarranted** pessimism (more than is reasonable or necessary)

**Nouns that often precede "pessimism" (as a type of):**
*   a **sense of** pessimism
*   a **wave of** pessimism
*   a **mood of** pessimism
*   a **streak of** pessimism (a tendency that appears sometimes)

---

## hegemony

### Meaning of "Hegemony"

**Hegemony** (pronounced "HEJ-uh-moh-nee" or "HEG-uh-moh-nee") refers to the **leadership or dominance, especially by one country or social group over others.**

It's more than just simple power or control. Here's a deeper dive:

1.  **Leadership & Dominance:** It signifies a commanding influence, typically by a state, an organization, or a social class, over others.
2.  **Consent & Coercion (Implicit/Explicit):** A key aspect of hegemony is that the dominant power doesn't always rely solely on overt force. It often involves a combination of:
    *   **Coercion:** The threat or use of force.
    *   **Consent:** Gaining the consent of the dominated groups, perhaps through the propagation of certain ideologies, cultural norms, or economic systems that appear beneficial or natural to everyone, even if they primarily serve the dominant power's interests. 3.  **Sphere of Influence:** This dominance can be political, economic, cultural, or ideological.
    *   **Political Hegemony:** One nation's leadership in international affairs.
    *   **Economic Hegemony:** One country or company's overwhelming control over a particular market or global economy.
    *   **Cultural Hegemony:** The pervasive influence of one culture's values, beliefs, and practices over others, often leading to their acceptance as universal norms.
    *   **Ideological Hegemony:** The dominance of a particular set of ideas or a worldview, often making alternative viewpoints seem marginal or irrational.

**In essence, hegemony is about a powerful entity setting the rules, shaping the discourse, and influencing the very way others think and operate, often without needing to constantly exert direct force.**

## **Pariah**

**Meaning:**
A **pariah** (pronounced puh-RY-uh) is a person who is **rejected from society or a social group**, an **outcast**, or someone who is **generally despised and avoided**.

The term originates from the **Paraiyar** (or Pariah) caste in southern India, who were historically considered "untouchables" and were at the very bottom of the social hierarchy, performing tasks deemed impure and facing severe social ostracism. While its origin is specific, its modern usage is broader and can refer to anyone who is treated as an outcast or shunned.

*   **Disgrace/Scorn:** Viewed with contempt or disapproval.
*   **Isolation:** Lacking social connection or acceptance.

*   **Treated as a pariah:** To be subjected to the kind of treatment an outcast receives.
*   **Viewed as a pariah:** To be perceived by others as an outcast.
*   **Social pariah:** An outcast within a general social setting (often redundant but emphasizes the social aspect).
*   **International pariah:** A country or leader widely condemned and isolated by the global community.
*   **Corporate pariah:** An individual or company shunned within the business world.
*   **Moral pariah:** Someone despised for their perceived lack of ethics or integrity.
*   **Political pariah:** A politician or political entity widely rejected and isolated.

## defamation

Okay, let's break down "defamation" for your GRE studies. This is an excellent word to know, especially for Reading Comprehension and Analytical Writing, as it often appears in legal, ethical, and media contexts.

---

### Understanding "Defamation"

**Meaning:**
**Defamation** (noun) refers to the act of making a **false statement** about someone that harms their **reputation**. It's about damaging a person's good name, character, or standing in the community through lies.

Key points:
*   **False Statement:** The statement must be untrue. If it's a truthful but damaging statement, it's generally not defamation (though it might be a breach of privacy in some cases).
*   **Harm to Reputation:** The false statement must actually damage the person's reputation, cause them to be ridiculed, shunned, or suffer financial loss.
*   **Communication to a Third Party:** The false statement must be communicated to at least one person other than the subject of the statement. You can't defame someone to their face if no one else hears it.
*   **Intent (in legal contexts):** In many legal systems, there's also a component of malice or negligence involved, especially for public figures.

There are two main types of defamation:
1.  **Libel:** Defamation in a **written** form (e.g., in newspapers, magazines, books, online articles, emails, social media posts).
2.  **Slander:** Defamation in a **spoken** form (e.g., during a speech, on a podcast, in a casual conversation).

**In simpler terms:** It's spreading harmful lies about someone.

---

### Common Collocations of "Defamation"

Collocations are words that frequently go together. Knowing these will help you use "defamation" naturally and recognize it in various contexts.

**Verbs that often precede "defamation":**
*   **commit defamation:** To perform the act of defaming.
*   **engage in defamation:** To participate in defaming someone.
*   **allege defamation:** To claim that defamation has occurred.
*   **sue for defamation:** To take legal action because of defamation.
*   **bring a claim/suit for defamation:** Similar to suing.
*   **accuse someone of defamation:** To state that someone has defamed another.
*   **prove defamation:** To legally establish that defamation took place.
*   **avoid defamation:** To take steps to not defame anyone.

**Adjectives that often describe "defamation":**
*   **baseless/unfounded defamation:** Defamation that has no factual basis.
*   **malicious defamation:** Defamation done with harmful intent.
*   **serious defamation:** Defamation with significant negative impact.
*   **widespread defamation:** Defamation that has reached a large audience.
*   **public defamation:** Defamation that occurs in the public sphere.

**Nouns/Phrases often associated with "defamation":**
*   **defamation of character:** A very common phrase emphasizing the attack on reputation.
*   **defamation lawsuit/suit/claim/case:** Legal proceedings related to defamation.
*   **act of defamation:** Referring to a specific instance of defaming.
*   **charge of defamation:** An accusation of defaming someone.

---

## gratuitous

Let's break down "gratuitous" for your GRE studies. It's a fantastic word to know because it often appears in contexts of criticism or analysis.

---

### Meaning of "Gratuitous"

"Gratuitous" has two main meanings, though one is far more common in modern usage and for tests like the GRE:

1.  **Unnecessary, unwarranted, or inappropriate:** This is the most common and often negative connotation. Something gratuitous in this sense is given, done, or displayed without good reason, often being excessive, uncalled for, or offensive. It adds nothing of value and often detracts.
    *   *Example:* Gratuitous violence in a film (violence that serves no artistic or narrative purpose, just there for shock value).
    *   *Think:* "Why is *that* there? It serves no purpose and feels out of place or excessive."

2.  **Given or done free of charge; without payment:** This meaning is closer to the word's Latin root "gratis" (meaning "free"). While technically correct, it's less commonly used today in casual conversation, often replaced by "free," "complimentary," or "pro bono" (for legal services).
    *   *Example:* Gratuitous services (services provided for free).
    *   *Think:* "It was provided without cost."

**For the GRE, focus overwhelmingly on the first meaning: unnecessary, unwarranted, or inappropriate.**

---

### Common Collocations

When you see "gratuitous," it frequently pairs with nouns describing something undesirable or superfluous:

*   **Gratuitous violence:** The most common one.
*   **Gratuitous nudity/sex:** Used to describe explicit content that doesn't advance the plot or character development.
*   **Gratuitous remark/comment/insult:** An uncalled-for or offensive statement.
*   **Gratuitous advice:** Unsolicited and often unhelpful or unwanted advice.
*   **Gratuitous detail/exposition:** Unnecessary information that clutters a story or explanation.
*   **Gratuitous act/display:** An action or show that is unprovoked or without good reason.

## hyperbole

---

### Understanding Hyperbole

**Definition:**
**Hyperbole** (pronounced: hy-PER-buh-lee) is a rhetorical device or figure of speech in which **exaggeration is used for emphasis or effect.** It is an overstatement that is **not intended to be taken literally**, but rather to create a strong impression, evoke humor, or highlight a point with dramatic flair.

*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   **Deliberate exaggeration:** It's a conscious choice by the speaker/writer.
    *   **Not meant to deceive:** The exaggeration is usually obvious and understood by the audience as non-literal.
    *   **Purposeful:** Used to add color, intensity, humor, or to persuade.

Think of it as stretching the truth to make a point, rather than to lie.

---

### Common Collocations for Hyperbole

Collocations are words that often go together. Knowing these will help you understand and use the word "hyperbole" more naturally.

*   **Verbs frequently used with "hyperbole":**
    *   **use hyperbole:** *The politician tends to use hyperbole in his speeches.*
    *   **employ hyperbole:** *Advertisers often employ hyperbole to make their products sound irresistible.*
    *   **engage in hyperbole:** *When he gets excited, he often engages in hyperbole.*

*   **Adjectives frequently describing "hyperbole":**
    *   **rhetorical hyperbole:** *The speech was full of rhetorical hyperbole, designed to stir emotions.*
    *   **sheer hyperbole:** *His claims were dismissed as sheer hyperbole, with no basis in fact.*
    *   **gross hyperbole:** *The media report contained gross hyperbole, distorting the truth.*
    *   **extreme hyperbole:** *The film critic's review was an example of extreme hyperbole, calling it both the best and worst movie ever.*
    *   **literary hyperbole:** *Shakespeare's plays are rich in literary hyperbole.*
    *   **political hyperbole:** *Campaigns are often characterized by political hyperbole from all sides.*

*   **Other common phrases:**
    *   **a bit of hyperbole:** *There was a bit of hyperbole in her description of the party.*
    *   **master of hyperbole:** *He's a master of hyperbole, making every small event sound like a monumental crisis.*

## dilemma

Okay, let's break down the word "dilemma" for your GRE studies.

---

## Dilemma

**Meaning:**

A **dilemma** is a situation in which a difficult choice has to be made between two or more alternatives, especially equally undesirable ones. It implies a "no-win" situation or a choice between "the lesser of two evils."

*   **Origin:** From Greek, combining "di-" (meaning "two") and "lemma" (meaning "proposition" or "premise"). This highlights the core idea of choosing between two difficult propositions.
*   **Key characteristic:** The difficulty arises because both (or all) options have significant drawbacks, or because choosing one means sacrificing something valuable from the other. It's not just *any* difficult choice, but one where neither option is clearly good.

---

**Common Collocations:**

Collocations are words that often go together. Knowing these will help you understand and use "dilemma" naturally.

**Verbs that go with dilemma:**

*   **Face a dilemma / Be in a dilemma:** To be confronted with or situated within a difficult choice.
    *   *Example:* She **faced a dilemma** when both job offers came with significant downsides.
*   **Present a dilemma / Pose a dilemma:** When a situation or fact creates the difficult choice.
    *   *Example:* The new regulations **present a dilemma** for small businesses.
*   **Resolve a dilemma / Solve a dilemma:** To find a way out of or make a decision in a difficult situation.
    *   *Example:* They worked hard to **resolve the dilemma** before the deadline.
*   **Grapple with a dilemma / Wrestle with a dilemma:** To struggle mentally or emotionally with a difficult choice.
    *   *Example:* He spent days **grappling with the dilemma** of whether to move abroad.
*   **Create a dilemma / Put someone in a dilemma:** To cause a difficult choice for someone.
    *   *Example:* His sudden resignation **put the manager in a dilemma**.

**Adjectives that describe dilemma:**

*   **Moral dilemma / Ethical dilemma:** A situation where a difficult choice must be made between two courses of action, both of which conflict with one's moral principles or ethical code.
    *   *Example:* The doctor faced a serious **moral dilemma**.
*   **Practical dilemma:** A difficult choice related to everyday actions or logistics.
    *   *Example:* Moving all the equipment presented a **practical dilemma**.
*   **Serious dilemma / Grave dilemma:** A very important or severe difficult choice.
*   **Awkward dilemma / Tricky dilemma:** A difficult choice that is also uncomfortable or hard to navigate.
*   **Classic dilemma:** A well-known or archetypal difficult choice (e.g., "head or heart").

---

**Example Sentences in Various Contexts (with Traditional Chinese Translations):**

1.  **Context: Personal Choice / Everyday Life**
    *   **English:** She **faced a classic dilemma**: either miss her best friend's wedding or fail her final exam, as both were scheduled for the same day.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 她面臨一個典型的**兩難困境**：要麼錯過她最好朋友的婚禮，要麼期末考試不及格，因為兩者都安排在同一天。

2.  **Context: Business / Economic**
    *   **English:** The company found itself **in a serious dilemma**: increase prices and risk losing customers, or absorb rising costs and face bankruptcy.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 這家公司陷入了一個嚴重的**困境**：要麼提高價格並冒著失去客戶的風險，要麼承受不斷上升的成本並面臨破產。

3.  **Context: Ethics / Professional**
    *   **English:** The doctor **grappled with a moral dilemma**, having to choose between respecting patient confidentiality and reporting a serious public health risk.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 醫生**苦思著一個道德兩難**，他必須在尊重病患的隱私和報告嚴重的公共健康風險之間做出選擇。

4.  **Context: Academic / Research**
    *   **English:** Researchers **were presented with a practical dilemma**: how to conduct the experiment without compromising the integrity of the data while staying within budget.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 研究人員**面臨著一個實際的兩難問題**：如何在不損害數據完整性的情況下，在預算內進行實驗。

5.  **Context: Social / Political**
    *   **English:** Policymakers are currently **wrestling with the dilemma** of how to stimulate economic growth without exacerbating inflation.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 政策制定者目前正**努力應對**如何刺激經濟增長而又不加劇通貨膨脹的**兩難局面**。

6.  **Context: Abstract / Existential**
    *   **English:** His unexpected inheritance **created an awkward dilemma** for him: pursue his dream of becoming an artist, or take over the family business as expected.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 他意外的遺產**為他製造了一個尷尬的兩難**：是追求成為藝術家的夢想，還是按照預期接管家族企業。

---

By understanding its core meaning, common collocations, and seeing it in varied contexts, you'll be well-prepared to identify and use "dilemma" effectively on the GRE!

## solicitous

That's an excellent word to know for the GRE! Let's break down "solicitous."

### Meaning of "Solicitous"

**"Solicitous"** (adjective) describes someone who shows **attentive care or concern**, often accompanied by a **degree of eagerness, anxiety, or worry**, especially regarding someone's well-being, comfort, or success, or about a particular outcome. It implies a proactive and often earnest desire to ensure something goes well or someone is taken care of.

Think of it as more than just "caring." It suggests a *proactive* and sometimes *anxious* form of care.

*   **Key aspects:**
    *   **Attentive concern:** You're paying close attention to someone's needs or a situation.
    *   **Eagerness/Worry:** This concern often comes with a desire for a positive outcome or apprehension about a negative one.
    *   **Proactive:** You're not just passively caring; you're actively trying to help or ensure well-being.

### Common Collocations

"Solicitous" typically takes specific prepositions:

*   **Solicitous for:** someone's well-being, safety, comfort, welfare, success.
    *   *Example:* She was solicitous *for* her children's future.
*   **Solicitous about:** a person, a situation, an outcome, details.
    *   *Example:* He was solicitous *about* his friend's performance on the exam.
*   **Solicitous of:** someone's needs, feelings, reputation (less common but valid).
    *   *Example:* The manager was solicitous *of* his employees' morale.

You'll also see it modifying nouns related to people or their actions:

*   A **solicitous parent/friend/host/attendant.**
*   **Solicitous inquiries/questions/glances/attention.**

### Example Sentences in Various Contexts

Here are multiple example sentences with their Traditional Chinese translations:

1.  **Context: Parental/Personal Care**
    *   **English:** A **solicitous** mother constantly checked on her feverish child throughout the night, ensuring he was comfortable.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 一位**憂心的**母親整夜不斷地查看她發燒的孩子，確保他感到舒適。
    *   *(Note: "憂心的" captures the anxious care well.)*

2.  **Context: Professional Service/Hospitality**
    *   **English:** The hotel staff was **solicitous** of our every need, making our stay very pleasant and relaxing.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 飯店員工對我們的每一個需求都**照顧周到**，讓我們的住宿非常愉快放鬆。
    *   *(Note: "照顧周到" conveys the attentive and proactive care.)*

3.  **Context: Concern about an Outcome/Situation**
    *   **English:** He was **solicitous** about his friend's job interview, sending encouraging texts all morning and waiting eagerly for news.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 他很**關心**他朋友的工作面試，整個早上都發送鼓勵的簡訊，並焦急地等待消息。
    *   *(Note: "關心" is a good general fit, but the "焦急地等待" reinforces the "solicitous" nuance.)*

4.  **Context: Overly Anxious/Potentially Intrusive Concern (Slightly Negative Connotation)**
    *   **English:** While his intentions were good, his overly **solicitous** questions about my personal life sometimes felt intrusive.

## truncate

Okay, let's break down the word "truncate" for your GRE preparation!

---

## **Truncate**

**Meaning:**

*   **Definition:** To shorten something by cutting off the top or end, or to remove a part of something. It often implies an abrupt or deliberate cutting or shortening.
*   **Etymology:** The word comes from the Latin "truncare," meaning "to cut off." This helps reinforce the core meaning.
*   **Key Idea:** Think of it as lopping off the end of something to make it shorter, rather than just "making something short."

**Common Collocations:**

"Truncate" often collocates with nouns referring to things that can be shortened, especially data, text, or less literally, life or careers.

*   **Truncate a file/data/string:** To shorten digital information.
*   **Truncate a name/word/text:** To shorten written or spoken content.
*   **Truncate a report/article/document:** To condense written material.
*   **Truncate a number:** In mathematics, to remove digits after a certain point without rounding (e.g., truncating 3.14159 to two decimal places gives 3.14).
*   **Truncate a tree/branch:** (Literal use) To cut off a part of a plant.
*   **Truncate a career/life:** (Figurative, often implying a premature or tragic end) To cut short.
*   **Truncate to [a specific length/value]:** To shorten something *to* a particular limit.
*   **Be truncated:** (Passive voice) Often used when something *is* shortened by an external process or event.

---

**Example Sentences with Traditional Chinese Translations:**

Here are multiple examples across various contexts:

1.  **Context: Shortening written content**
    *   **English:** The editor decided to **truncate** the article to fit the remaining space in the newspaper.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 編輯決定**截短**這篇文章，以符合報紙的剩餘版面空間。

2.  **Context: Limiting digital data/strings**
    *   **English:** When displaying file names, the software will automatically **truncate** them if they exceed 25 characters.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 顯示檔案名稱時，如果檔案名稱超過25個字元，軟體會將其自動**截斷**。

3.  **Context: Figurative use (life/career)**
    *   **English:** His promising acting career was tragically **truncated** by a sudden illness.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 他前途光明的演藝生涯因一場突如其來的疾病而不幸地被**截斷**了。

4.  **Context: Mathematical operations**
    *   **English:** Unlike rounding, **truncating** a number like 3.14159 to two decimal places simply removes the last three digits, resulting in 3.14.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 與四捨五入不同，將3.14159這樣的數字**截斷**到小數點後兩位，只是簡單地移除最後三位數字，結果為3.14。

5.  **Context: Physical cutting (less common for GRE but good for understanding)**
    *   **English:** To encourage bushier growth, it's sometimes necessary to **truncate** the upper branches of a young tree.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 為了促進更茂密的生長，有時有必要**截短**幼樹的上層樹枝。

6.  **Context: Limiting displayed information on a user interface**
    *   **English:** Due to limited screen space, long user names will be **truncated** with an ellipsis (...) at the end.
    *   **Traditional Chinese:** 由於螢幕空間有限，較長的使用者名稱將在末尾被**截斷**並顯示省略符號（...）。

---

**GRE Tip:**
"Truncate" is a relatively formal verb. In the GRE, you might see it in reading comprehension passages discussing technology, data, or even abstract concepts like time or opportunity being cut short. Understanding its precise meaning—cutting off an end to shorten—is key.
